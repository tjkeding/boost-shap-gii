 <research_conducted>
Seven research agents (research-agent-sonnet-high) dispatched in parallel to verify domain-load-bearing claims against current literature. Returned structured JSON verdicts:

1. **GII = sqrt(M × V) composite (geometric mean of mean-|SHAP| and SHAP-vs-feature spline-SD).** Verdict: inconclusive; literature state: speculative. No published precedent for this exact composite. Mean-|SHAP| is a widely used global importance metric (Lundberg &amp; Lee 2017; Molnar 2022). SHAP variability/dispersion is recognized as conveying complementary information but is typically reported as variance or standard deviation alone, not multiplied geometrically with magnitude. Geometric means presume comparable scales for the multiplicands; M (units of model output) and V (units of model output, but on a fundamentally different statistic) are not on commensurate footing. The composite has no validated calibration against ground-truth feature-importance benchmarks.

2. **Boruta-style shadow features for SHAP null calibration.** Verdict: inconclusive; literature state: emerging. The Boruta algorithm (Kursa &amp; Rudnicki 2010) uses shadow features for permutation-importance null calibration in random forests; BorutaShap (Keany 2020, GitHub) extends this to SHAP for tree models. Neither has formal Type-I error control at any nominal alpha; both rely on the shadow-permutation null being a valid approximation to the no-effect null. The stratified max-shadow-per-stratum extension implemented here is not present in published Boruta-SHAP literature and lacks coverage analysis.

3. **Percentile bootstrap labeled as "Efron-tier."** Verdict: contradicted; literature state: consensus. The percentile method (Efron 1979; Efron &amp; Tibshirani 1993) is first-order accurate (coverage error O(n^-1/2)). BCa (Efron 1987) and bootstrap-t are second-order accurate (O(n^-1)) and are preferred for asymmetric or small-sample distributions. The label "Efron-tier" is not a standard term and conflates the method (percentile) with the author. For SHAP-magnitude statistics that are non-negative and right-skewed, BCa or studentized intervals would yield better-calibrated coverage; the choice of percentile is defensible only as a simple default, not as a methodologically optimal choice.

4. **BH-FDR applied to stratified shadow-exceedance p-values.** Verdict: contradicted; literature state: consensus. Benjamini-Hochberg (1995) controls FDR at nominal alpha under independence or positive regression dependence on the subset (PRDS; Benjamini &amp; Yekutieli 2001). The dependence structure of stratified-shadow exceedance p-values across features sharing the same shadow null is not PRDS in any verifiable sense; the shadow nulls are themselves correlated through the joint training procedure. Benjamini-Yekutieli (BY, Benjamini &amp; Yekutieli 2001) controls FDR under arbitrary dependence at the cost of a log(m)-factor reduction in power; it is the conservative-correct choice when PRDS cannot be established.

5. **MAD-based Huber delta = 1.345 × 1.4826 × MAD(y).** Verdict: supported; literature state: consensus. The constant 1.4826 makes MAD a consistent estimator of sigma under Gaussianity (Hampel 1974; Rousseeuw &amp; Croux 1993). The 1.345 multiplier achieves 95% asymptotic efficiency at the Gaussian (Huber 1964). The product is the canonical choice. The IQR/1.3489 fallback when MAD = 0 is also standard (1.3489 ≈ z_{0.75} − z_{0.25}, the quantile-based Gaussian-consistency factor).

6. **LSQUnivariateSpline LSQBivariateSpline as V-component estimator with TV-based energy gate.** Verdict: inconclusive; literature state: speculative. LSQ-prefix scipy splines fit a B-spline basis at user-supplied knots via least squares (de Boor 1978; Dierckx 1993). The 2D bivariate spline as implemented does NOT separately model main effects and interaction in the SS-ANOVA smoothing-spline-ANOVA decomposition (Wahba 1990; Gu 2013); it fits the joint surface directly. The total-variation energy gate as a stability heuristic is not a standard literature device for spline regression diagnostics; rejection thresholds are heuristic.

7. **OOB-vs-deployed-ensemble CI methodology for per-individual SHAP.** Verdict: inconclusive; literature state: emerging. Out-of-bag confidence interval methods for ensemble predictions (Wager et al. 2014, jackknife-after-bootstrap; Mentch &amp; Hooker 2016, infinitesimal jackknife for random forests) provide valid OOB-based variance estimation. None of these has been extended formally to SHAP per-individual estimates. The ensemble-replicate inference-time CI approach (K refits per bootstrap iteration b sharing the SAME bootstrap sample) does NOT mimic the deployed K-fold-ensemble's training-data variability; the deployed ensemble uses K DIFFERENT fold splits, while the inference CI uses one bootstrap sample replicated K times. The construction is internally consistent only if the user's inferential target is "ensemble averaged over K refits each fit on bootstrap b" rather than "ensemble averaged over K fold-specific models trained on the deployed splits."

 <finding id="finding-5" severity="critical" category="scientific_rigor">
 <location file="src/boost_shap_gii/shap_utils.py" lines="GII = sqrt(M * V) composite" />
 <description>The pipeline's central metric, GII = sqrt(M × V), is a geometric mean of two quantities with different statistical interpretations: M is mean of |SHAP| (a first-moment magnitude on the model-output scale), and V is the standard deviation of a fitted spline of SHAP vs. feature value (a measure of the trend's amplitude on the same scale, but indexing variation along the feature axis rather than across observations). Geometric means are sensible composites when the multiplicands are positive, dimensionally homogeneous, and on comparable scales. The two are dimensionally homogeneous (both in model-output units), but their numerical magnitudes are not benchmarked to be comparable; a dataset where M dominates V (or vice versa) yields a GII that is dominated by one component. No literature precedent validates this composite, and no calibration study against ground-truth-known feature importance has been performed.</description>
 <evidence>shap_utils.py computes `gii = np.sqrt(M * V)` and applies the same significance-gate logic to GII, M, and V independently. Research agent verdict 1: inconclusive, speculative.</evidence>
 <literature>Lundberg &amp; Lee 2017 NeurIPS (foundational SHAP); Lundberg et al. 2020 Nat. Mach. Intell. (SHAP for trees); Aas et al. 2021 Artif. Intell. (SHAP for correlated features). Mean-|SHAP| is the standard global-importance summary; SD-of-SHAP-vs-feature is uncommon, and no publication uses sqrt(M × V) as a published global-importance index.</literature>
 <impact>Defensibility hazard at peer review. A reviewer can ask: "What does the geometric mean of magnitude and variability optimize? Why not arithmetic mean, or weighted sum, or just M or V separately?" Without a justification rooted in a decision-theoretic objective or empirical calibration, the composite is hard to defend. If one of M or V is much larger than the other, GII essentially tracks the dominant component, and the composite adds noise rather than information.</impact>
 <recommendation>Either (a) provide a calibration study with synthetic data of known feature importance, demonstrating that GII discriminates true positives from null features better than M or V alone; or (b) demote GII from a published index to an exploratory display, and report M and V as the primary quantities; or (c) anchor GII to a decision-theoretic framework (e.g., "GII represents the geometric mean trade-off between magnitude-based importance and trend-based interpretability"). Without one of these, the central metric should not appear unqualified in publication.</recommendation>
 </finding>

 <finding id="finding-7" severity="major" category="validity">
 <location file="src/boost_shap_gii/shap_utils.py" lines="calculate_v_spline_2d" />
 <description>The 2D V-component fit uses `LSQBivariateSpline` directly on the joint (feature_a, feature_b, SHAP_interaction) scatter without separating main-effect contributions. In SS-ANOVA smoothing-spline-ANOVA decompositions (Wahba 1990; Gu 2013), the bivariate fit is decomposed as f(x,y) = f_0 + f_1(x) + f_2(y) + f_{12}(x,y), with the interaction term f_{12} subject to an identifiability constraint that orthogonalizes it against the main-effects subspace. The current implementation fits the joint surface without this decomposition, so the resulting "V" includes contributions from feature_a's and feature_b's main-effect curvatures and is not a pure interaction-amplitude measure.</description>
 <evidence>Direct `LSQBivariateSpline.__call__` with no orthogonalization; the result's TV/SD is reported as V for interaction effects.</evidence>
 <literature>Wahba 1990 SIAM (smoothing splines); Gu 2013 Springer (SS-ANOVA monograph); Lee &amp; Wahba 1979 JASA (additive vs. interaction smoothing splines). SHAP interaction values themselves are conceptualized as the interaction contribution after main-effect removal (Lundberg et al. 2020), but the V-component re-introduces main-effect contamination by fitting a non-decomposed bivariate spline.</literature>
 <impact>V for interaction effects is biased upward when either feature_a or feature_b has a strong main-effect SHAP curvature, because the bivariate spline absorbs that curvature into its fit. This conflates "interaction is strongly varying" with "main effects are strongly varying"; significance gates may flag pairs with strong main effects but weak true interaction.</impact>
 <recommendation>Either (a) implement SS-ANOVA decomposition (e.g., via `pygam` or a hand-rolled basis subtraction); or (b) constrain the bivariate spline to satisfy approximate marginal-zero conditions; or (c) explicitly document that V_interaction conflates main-effect curvature and interpret accordingly. Option (a) is the methodologically rigorous path.</recommendation>
 </finding>
